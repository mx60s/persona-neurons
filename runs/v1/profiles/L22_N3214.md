## 2. Activation

### Base (observations)

- **Negative, in assistant replies (strongest).** The base response goes negative in assistant replies at positions where the reply acknowledges, contains or names a difficulty for the user:
  - "I am [unable] to have data structures" -7.75 (e09, validation); "I am [unable] to create a video tutorial" -7.47 (e15)
  - "I bet it's [tough]" -4.91 (e10, validation)
  - "taking classes in the summer [can] really stink" -4.72 (e11)
  - "Job interviews [are] always nerve-wracking" -3.83 (e14)
  - "Oh wow, introducing yourself to [strangers] sounds incredibly anxiety-inducing" -4.81 (t04); "can be quite [overwhelming]" -5.47 (t05)

  Live, the same sentence "Job interviews are always nerve-wracking." gives -5.25 at "always" as an assistant reply, +0.15 in the user turn, and +0.49 as plain text.
- **Negative, outside replies.** Advice-register text also goes negative: "As easy as they are to care for[,] there are still some best practices" -7.91 (e08, a chat-doc continuation); "your knowledge of the process is [quite] limited" -2.31 (e13, web, validation).
- **Positive (weaker, max +3.1).** Non-reply text that describes a difficulty, usually at concessive links:
  - "still below pre-2008 levels[,] but the rebound" +3.11 (e00)
  - "stuck between my friends and my family[,] and I don't wanna disappoint" +3.02 (e01)
  - "searched for signs of life ... [but] there was no one" +2.59 (e04)
  - "a really unpleasant [job]" +2.39 (e06, validation)
  - training t00: "another huge stressor ... capital to get started[,] and there's always the risk" +7.5
- **Near zero.** Factual and code text, and random or quantile windows (e22, e23, e25, e29, e31: |a| < 0.4). Anxiety vocabulary alone does nothing. Live: "Anxiety disorders affect about four percent of people ... persistent worry." gives +0.05 at "worry" as plain text, and +0.19 when given as an informational assistant reply.
- **Lens of the down column.** A positive activation promotes "很难" (very hard), "难以" (hard to), "很差", "questionable", "没有办法" (no way), "混乱" (chaos), "suffers", "uncertain". A negative one promotes "只需要" (only need), "的好处" (benefit), "momentarily". At layer 22 the lens is only suggestive; the next-token tests in section 7 are the stronger evidence.

### How each adapter changes it (observations)

- **In assistant-reply tokens (597 tokens in the stored windows):** tokens with base below p01 (mean -2.27) go to +0.11 under n_amp+1 and -3.35 under n_sup+1. Tokens with base above p99 (mean +1.87) go to 2.38 and 0.59. Linear fit: n_amp ≈ 0.47·base + 0.53, n_sup ≈ 1.04·base - 0.51, ctrl ≈ 0.93·base - 0.03 (r 0.99). So in replies the amplifier adds a positive offset that roughly cancels the base negative response, the suppressor adds a negative offset, and the control does nothing.
- **Outside replies (911 tokens):** n_amp ≈ 1.02·base + 0.03 and n_sup ≈ 0.88·base - 0.04. Small changes only.
- **Corpus means by domain:** emotional_chat +0.064 (amp) / -0.102 (sup). Factual -0.041 / +0.027, web -0.027 / +0.014, code -0.029 / +0.017. Away from chat the small shifts reverse sign, which is why the corpus mean change is near zero for both adapters even though the token-level changes are large and opposite.
- **Trigger vs magnitude vs sign.** The trigger is unchanged: the same reply positions are affected. The response is offset, often enough to flip the sign under n_amp (B1 "hard": -3.91 base, +1.28 amp, -6.22 sup).
- **Live examples:**
  - reply "I am unable to create a video tutorial": -7.56 / -4.16 / -9.06 / -7.41 (base / amp / sup / ctrl)
  - reply "That sounds really stressful. Interviews can be so hard." (span mean): -3.80 / +0.58 / -5.10 / -3.51
  - replies with positive content shrink under amp and grow slightly under sup ("That's wonderful news. Congratulations ..." span mean +0.55 / +0.29 / +0.62)

**Answer to the key question.** The amplifier pushes up and the suppressor pushes down in assistant-reply text, almost entirely at positions where base already responds negatively (acknowledging or naming a difficulty, refusing, apologizing, reassuring). In non-chat text the shifts are small and slightly reversed, which cancels the corpus mean. So the context-dependence follows the base response: both adapters act on a response that exists in base, and the control leaves it alone.

## 3. Explanation and competing explanations

Hypothesis: in an assistant reply, a negative activation marks a position where the assistant voice is about to name, qualify or contain a difficulty, and it favours downtoning continuations (" a" [bit], " some", " slightly", " exciting", " normal") over intensifiers (" really", " so", " extremely"). The neuroticism amplifier offsets this response toward zero or positive (intensifier-favouring). The suppressor deepens it (downtoner-favouring). Outside replies, a weaker positive mode marks concessive or difficulty statements in third-person or narrative text.

Competing explanations:

- (a) An "assistant-voice register" feature with no difficulty content. Partly supported: the neutral reply B2 ("That sounds like a good plan. Interviews can be an hour long.") reached -5.6 at "can be", and e08 (plant-care instructions) reached -7.9. Against: the positive-news reply F2 (+0.55 span mean) and "I am happy to create a written tutorial" (+0.48) are not negative.
- (b) A next-token feature for "difficulty adjective coming next" (stressful, tough, nerve-wracking), with sign set by speaker. This fits B2, where "can be" anticipates "stressful". I did not separate it from the main hypothesis.
- (c) Automated card (DeepSeek): "negative on modal/auxiliary verbs after a comma". Rejected: its own tests failed 9 of 10 (rule v2), and in my tests the reply context matters far more than the part of speech (A1 vs A3).

- Next-token effects (live; U → reply prefix):
  - "It's completely normal to feel" (P(" nervous") = 1.0, so no top-token change): add +0.05 raises the log-prob of " really", " extremely", " incredibly", " super" (+1.6 to +2.1) and lowers " a", " some", " slightly" (-1.9 to -3.0). Add -0.05 does the reverse.

## 8. Unresolved

- B2 and e08: strongly negative reply or advice positions with no difficulty in the user's message. Is the trigger "assistant about to say a difficulty adjective" (competing explanation (b)) rather than "acknowledging the user's difficulty"?
- Why positive-content replies show the reversed adapter pattern (amp lowers, sup raises) while negative-content replies show amp up and sup down.
- Why the small shifts in factual, web and code text are reversed relative to replies. This reversal is what cancels the corpus mean.

Next test: behavioral zero ablation of this neuron in n_sup+1 (20 prompts, layer-matched random control), scoring judged neuroticism and counting downtoners ("a bit", "slightly", "a little") in replies. Section 7 predicts fewer downtoners if the neuron contributes to the suppressor's style.

## 9. Status

**tested.** The base response is negative at difficulty-acknowledging positions in assistant replies, near zero for anxiety vocabulary in informational text and for positive replies, and weakly positive for difficulty narration outside replies. The adapter change is an opposite offset in reply context, absent in the control. The output direction (intensifier vs downtoner) is confirmed by next-token interventions above the random control. 7 of 12 grouped tests pass, 4 are mixed and 1 fails (B: neutral reply also negative). Single-neuron behavioral effects are within the random range.
