# L2/N8295 — detailed profile (review, claude-opus-5-5)

Status: **tested** for the adapter-change claim; the base trigger is described only as a token list.

## 1. ID, selection, robustness

- Layer 2 (of 36), neuron 8295. Activation = `silu(gate) * up` before `down_proj`.
- Selected as openness **shared**, rank 1: "large change under both poles of this trait, small under the other trait".
- Effects on the 201k-token corpus (z = change / base RMS 0.0559):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| o_amp+1 | +0.23 | 0.60 | 1.00 | 0.62 |
| o_sup+1 | -0.14 | 0.42 | 0.98 | 0.41 |
| n_amp+1 | -0.03 | 0.08 | 0.98 | 0.12 |
| n_sup+1 | -0.01 | 0.07 | 0.43 | 0.08 |
| ctrl+1 | +0.04 | 0.08 | 1.00 | 0 |

- Token-level correlation of the amp and sup changes: **-0.97** for openness (-0.19 for neuroticism).
- Robustness: the top 0.1% of tokens carry only 2.5% (amp) and 3.8% (sup) of the squared change. Trimmed z_rms is 0.59 / 0.41, and the trimmed correlation is -0.97. This is not driven by outliers.
- Trajectory over the amplifier coefficient is linear: z_mean -0.18, -0.10, 0, +0.11, +0.23 for -1, -0.5, 0, +0.5, +1.
- Own weights barely move: 1-cos is 2.7e-5 for the gate row and 4.5e-6 for the down column under o_amp.
- Base distribution: p01 -0.191, p50 +0.010, p99 +0.159, p999 +0.316, max 0.75. 70% of tokens are positive. The neuron is small in absolute terms.

## 2. Activation

### Base model (observations)

- **Positive side.** It fires on specific whole-word or final-piece tokens, without a shared meaning. Corpus examples: ` restroom` 0.75 (e00), `brities` in "Celebrities" 0.69 (e01), ` multiprocessing` in an import list 0.67 (e02), ` getLogger` 0.65 (e03), ` Huckabee` after "Sarah" 0.61/0.54 (e04, e06), `noxious` 0.57 (e05), ` clinging` 0.54 (e07). The top_diff windows add ` December` 0.45 (e15), `Objective` as a heading 0.49 (e16) and ` restless` 0.39 (e19). Training windows add ` delicate`, ` eternity`, `DllImport`, `ability` in "unpredictability" and ` dependable` (0.45 to 0.64).
- **Negative side.** It goes negative on common given names, at the first-name position: ` Willie` -0.49/-0.48 (e08, e09), ` Sharon` -0.45 (e10), ` Daniel` -0.45 (e11), ` Manuel` -0.45 (e12), ` Elizabeth` -0.27 (e28). It is also negative on `_literals` in `unicode_literals` (-0.44, e13).
- **Middle of the range** (quantile and random windows e21 to e31): values are -0.3 to +0.3 and scattered over content words, for example ` weapon` +0.24, ` compelling` -0.29, ` forget` -0.20. No domain stands out. Code, factual, narrative and chat all contribute to both tails.
- Context matters somewhat. ` multiprocessing` is 0.55 in an import list but 0.11 in prose (R5). ` Huckabee` is 0.57 after a first name but 0.28 alone (R8).

### How the adapters change it (observations)

- **o_amp+1 adds a near-constant offset to the neuron's gate pre-activation at every token.** In the saved windows (1,127 tokens) the gate shift is +0.164, with an SD of only 0.025. The up shift is +0.033 (SD 0.010). The gate term accounts for nearly all of the activation change: RMS 0.037 of 0.039, against 0.004 for the up term. **o_sup+1 shifts the gate by -0.084** (SD 0.035).
- Because the gate is usually far negative (median -3.8), the same offset changes the activation in proportion to the token's existing gate/up values. Strong positive tokens gain about +0.15 to +0.21 under amp and lose about the same under sup. Strong negative tokens (names) change by only +0.02 to +0.05. So the result is a change in **magnitude on the positive side**. It is not a new trigger and not a sign flip.
- The change is present in every window of every domain. Per-window mean changes run +0.01 to +0.03 (amp) and -0.006 to -0.026 (sup). The by-domain mean change under amp ranges from +0.009 (emotional_chat) to +0.017 (chat), and under sup from -0.004 to -0.011. Prompt and continuation are similar (+0.011 vs +0.013).
- The neuroticism adapters and the neutral control **do not do this**. Their RMS change is about 1/8 to 1/10 of o_amp's on every text tested live.

## 3. Explanation and competing explanations

- **Explanation (hypothesis).** In the base model this is a lexical early-layer unit: positive on a scattered set of word tokens, negative on given names. The openness adapters do not change what it responds to. They add a fixed bias to its gate input, positive for the amplifier and negative for the suppressor. That is why the amp and sup changes are almost perfectly anti-correlated token by token (-0.97): both are the same per-token sensitivity times a constant of opposite sign. Nothing in the change depends on openness-related content.
- **Competing explanation 1.** The offset is content-dependent but looks constant because the corpus is mostly not openness-related. Tested in R3 and R9: open-minded vs closed-minded framing gave relative amp gains of +0.26 vs +0.29, and worried vs calm text gave +0.38 vs +0.38. This explanation is not supported.
- **Competing explanation 2.** The offset comes from upstream changes (layers 0 and 1, or layer-2 attention) adding a constant to the residual stream, not from the gate-row LoRA. My data cannot separate these. Both give a near-constant gate shift. See the next test.
- DeepSeek's automated label was "positive: import-like words in code; negative: proper names". Its import-word half fails: prose ` multiprocessing` is weak, and most top tokens are not code. Its proper-name half is closer, but only first names are negative. Surnames (` Huckabee`, ` Buckley`) are positive.

## 5. Held-out check (validation-split windows)

The ten validation windows are e00, e01, e03, e04, e06, e09, e10, e23, e28 and e29.
- **Adapter rule** (amp adds and sup subtracts about 0.13 to 0.20 at strong positive tokens; names change by < 0.06; window corr(amp, sup) <= -0.9): 10/10 pass. Window correlations are -0.93 to -0.99. At the focus tokens, the positive windows show dAmp +0.13 to +0.20 and dSup -0.11 to -0.18. The name windows (e09 ` Willie`, e10 ` Sharon`, e28 ` Elizabeth`) show dAmp +0.02 to +0.05.
- **Base rule** (given names negative; whole-word content tokens positive): it fits e09, e10 and e28 (names) and e00, e01, e03, e04 and e06 (content tokens). It cannot say in advance which content tokens will be the strong ones, so it predicts sign more than magnitude.

## 6. Training examples (attribution level: engages the response / exposes a learned difference)

- Top base activations in training responses come from both teachers. Amplifying-teacher examples: ` delicate` 0.63 (t00, train_index 1074) and ` eternity` 0.60 (t01, 1547). Suppressing-teacher examples: `DllImport` 0.64 (t02, 616) and `ability` 0.52 (t03, 376). These examples engage the response. The response is lexical and appears in code and in personal prose alike.
- The largest-change training examples (t04 to t08: ` eternity`, `Political`, ` Professional`, ` amplify`, ` dependable`) show the same thing. The adapter changes the neuron's response on these examples by the usual offset (+0.17 to +0.21 under amp, -0.15 to -0.19 under sup), whether the text came from the amplifying teacher (t04, t05, t07) or the suppressing teacher (t06, t08).
- **training_selectivity:** in the base model, amplifying-teacher text and suppressing-teacher text drive the neuron equally (diff_z -0.05). Under o_amp both teacher means rise by about +0.02, and under o_sup both fall by about 0.013, so the diff stays near zero (-0.01 and -0.07). This is **neither pre-existing selectivity nor new selectivity**: the adapters change the neuron's overall level, not which text drives it.

## 7. Interventions

- **Natural range:** p01 -0.191 to p99 +0.159 in activation units. In residual units, p99 is 0.0103 of the median residual norm (resid_p50 = 16.0; down-column norm 1.04; add +1 unit = +15.4 activation). The adapter's change at strong tokens (about +0.2 activation) is about 0.013 residual units.
- **Behavioral steering/ablation** (20 openness prompts; change in judged trait score / coherence vs the unmodified state; dose k x 0.191):

| condition | L2/N8295 | random control L2/N9325 |
|---|---|---|
| base add -4x | +0.25 / +0.15 | +0.05 / 0.00 |
| base add -2x | -0.10 / +0.05 | +0.15 / -0.05 |
| base add +2x | +0.10 / 0.00 | +0.20 / +0.05 |
| base add +4x | +0.25 / -0.10 | +0.05 / -0.05 |
| base zero | +0.05 / +0.05 | -0.05 / 0.00 |
| o_amp+1 zero | +0.10 / +0.05 | +0.20 / +0.05 |

  All values fall inside the random-control range. Caveat: the matched random neuron is nearly dead (natural scale 0.00037), so its doses are effectively zero. Its rows show judge and generation noise of up to ±0.2. Responses diverged from the unmodified text on 12 to 18 of 20 prompts even for that near-dead control, so text divergence alone is not evidence of an effect.
- **Next-token effect (live, base, chat prompt "Tell me about your favorite food.", prefix "I love tamago, which can be so")**, add applied at all positions:
  - Adding +0.013 residual units (the size of the adapter's change): KL 0.0003, top-5 unchanged.
  - Adding +0.13 (10x; large push): KL 0.017. ` versatile` rises from 0.257 to 0.311 and ` many` falls from 0.480 to 0.400. The largest log-prob gains are small (+0.57 for ` intriguing`).
- No steering excerpts are shown: at doses near the natural range nothing is visible, and the neuron's output scale is tiny.
- **Patching:** group level only. This neuron is in the openness shared set (10 neurons). Patching o_amp's activations into base for that set recovered 0.65% of o_amp's log-likelihood advantage (CI 0.28% to 1.03%).

## 8. Unresolved cases and next test

- Unresolved:
  - Why ` multiprocessing` and ` Huckabee` depend on context (preceding `import` or first name).
  - Why surnames are positive but first names negative.
  - Why the gate/up lens tokens do not fire.
  - Where the constant gate offset comes from.
- **Next test:** compute the openness adapters' gate-row delta (`dW_gate[8295] @ x`) separately from the change in the layer-2 MLP input (`W_gate[8295] @ (x_state - x_base)`) on the same tokens. This shows whether the +0.16 offset comes from the neuron's own LoRA row or from upstream changes to the residual stream. If it is the own row, also project that row onto the mean residual direction at layer 2.

## 9. Status

**Tested.** The adapter-change hypothesis (a constant gate offset of opposite sign for amp and sup, magnitude only, no trigger change, specific to the openness pair) predicted all 18 live texts and all 10 validation windows. The base-trigger description ("lexical: scattered word tokens positive, given names negative") is weaker. Two of nine base tests failed or were mixed. It describes a token list, not a concept. Nothing here ties the neuron to openness content. It behaves like a per-adapter bias shift in an early lexical unit.
