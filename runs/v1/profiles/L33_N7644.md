# L33/N7644: "on"-particle predictor plus a chat-template role-token response

Status: **tested**. The base explanation (negative branch) and the adapter change both survived matched tests and held-out windows. The positive branch is still unexplained. The adapter change is not trait content. It is a near-constant shift at a single template position, with no measurable effect on the next token.

All activation values are the input to `down_proj` (silu(gate) * up). Base quantiles on the 201k-token corpus: p001 -16.75, p01 -0.70, p50 0.61, p99 1.78, p999 2.38; min -94, max +31.75. Live measurements were taken on 2026-10-26 on the explorer server (Qwen3-8B @ b968826, bf16, adapters @ 507053d).

## 2. Activation

### Base

**Negative branch (observation).** Very large negative values occur on the word right before a particle or preposition that the context makes highly predictable, usually " on":

- "Joke['s] on you" -92.5 (e08)
- "is [distributed] on an AS IS basis" -82.5 / -79.0 (e09, e10)
- "we shall [fight] on the beaches" -75.5 (e11, validation)
- "her students [hung] on her every word" -66.5 (e12)
- "fell [flat] on his face" -57.3 (e13, validation)
- training window t07: "like you're [walking] on eggshells" -139

The lens of the down column confirms the direction: a negative activation promotes " on", "-on", " On", ".on" and so on. Zeroing the neuron at these positions lowers P(" on") (section 4, T11). Magnitude tracks how expected " on" is, not the topic:

- "I'm so anxious that I feel like I'm walking" -22.0, P(on) 0.82
- "I'm so happy that I feel like I'm walking" -39.3, P(on) 0.96 ("walking on air")
- the same "walking" in a user turn -5.7, P(on) 0.07
- in an assistant reply -18.4, P(on) 0.61

Short contexts usually give much smaller values ("her students hung" in a 9-token sentence: -0.9). The stored 37-token windows reproduce the corpus magnitudes live (e12 window alone: -56.5).

**Positive branch (observation; not explained).** The largest positive values are also at positions before " on" or "forth":

- "learn new skills and [take] on" +31.9 (e00)
- "the gene is [switched] on" +25.3 (e01, validation)
- "[depending] on" +16.0 / +6.3 (e02, e04)
- "and [so] on" +7.2 (e03); "and [so] forth" +3.7 (e05)
- training: "2. [Turn] on your headlights" +29.4 (t00)

Gate is positive in both branches. The sign comes from `up`: about +3.4 at "take" versus about -10 to -12 at "distributed" or "'s" (detail file gate/up arrays). Zeroing at "take" did not change P(" on") (0.814 to 0.818). I cannot say what separates the positive from the negative " on" sites. The live test of "and so" gave only +0.70, below p99.

**Bulk of the distribution.** 94.6% of tokens are positive, with a median of 0.61. Quantile and random windows (e24 to e34) sit between 0 and 2 on ordinary tokens in every domain (narrative, web, code, chat). Nothing distinguishes them.

**Chat role token (observation).** The `assistant` token in `<|im_start|>assistant` gets -7.8 to -27 in base (e14 to e21 top_diff windows; e23 and e29 contain it too). Live, across seven different user messages (factual, code, anxious user, happy user, a user describing a sister's anxiety, "how do you feel", a nervous-persona roleplay), base ranged from -13.8 to -23.4 with no pattern by content. The response is specific to position:

- the first `assistant` after a user turn: -19.8
- `assistant` in a plain-text transcript ("assistant: Canberra."): +0.66
- `user` role token: about +0.6
- `<|im_start|>assistant` at the start of a sequence: +1.1
- second assistant turn in a two-turn chat: +0.73 in base (n_sup+1: -8.3)

### How each adapter changes it

- **Chat role token: magnitude, not trigger.** Across the seven user messages, n_amp+1 moves the value to -1.3 to -14.6 (mean shift about +12). n_sup+1 moves it to -37 to -50 (mean shift about -24). ctrl+1 stays within 3 of base, o_amp+1 matches base, and o_sup+1 is slightly less negative. Neither the shift nor its sign depends on what the user said. The change goes through the gate: at e14 to e21, gate is about 3.2 in base, about 1.0 under n_amp+1, and about 5.7 under n_sup+1. `up` moves less (-8.3 to -6.9 / -9.6). This single position produces the corpus-level prompt-segment pattern: mean change in prompt segments +0.20 (amp), -0.45 (sup), -0.06 (ctrl); continuation segments about 0.
- **"on"-prediction sites: small and inconsistent changes.** In my tests, n_amp+1 made the negative values 10 to 40% deeper in 4 of 7 cases and n_sup+1 made them 5 to 20% shallower in 6 of 7 (e.g. assistant-reply "walking": base -18.4, amp -25.5, sup -15.2). At the extreme corpus sites, all states move by under 10% (e09: -82.5 / -80.0 / -77.5).
- **Control:** matches base at the role token and at "on" sites.

**Answer to the key question for this neuron.** The amp and sup push in opposite directions in essentially one context: the first assistant role token of a chat, where base already responds strongly. The base response does not depend on the user's content, and neither do the pushes. Elsewhere, changes are small and not consistently opposite.

## 3. Explanation and competing explanations

Hypothesis (current): the neuron combines two unrelated functions.

1. A late-layer next-token feature. A negative activation promotes " on" when an idiom or phrasal construction makes " on" (or a similar particle) likely. There is also an unexplained positive mode at other " on" sites.
2. A large negative response at the first assistant role token of a chat. Its downstream use is unknown: zeroing it does not change the first reply token (section 7). The two neuroticism adapters scale this response in opposite directions (amp about x0.3, sup about x2.3). The control does not.

Competing explanations:

- (a) The role-token response is part of a high-norm or attention-sink mechanism at the start of the reply. On that view, the adapters change how strongly the reply start is marked, a recipe or persona-level signal rather than anything about anxious wording. Not tested.
- (b) Automated card (DeepSeek): "fires negatively on the particle 'on'". This is the right construction but the wrong token: the neuron fires one token earlier, before " on" is visible, and the card's tests measured at " on" itself.
- (c) Gemini card: "negative sentiment and possessiveness". Contradicted: "'s" in "Joke's on you" works through the " on" expectation, and "My sister is so anxious that she feels like she's drowning" gives +1.7.

## 5. Held-out check (validation windows)

- e01 "switched", e04 "depending", e11 "fight", e13 "flat": all four extreme validation windows are followed by " on" (4/4 for the trigger). Two are positive and two negative, and my explanation does not predict the sign.
- The `assistant` token appears in e13, e19, e21 and e29. In all four, n_amp+1 raises it (+13.3, +17.7, +19.3, +14.5) and n_sup+1 lowers it (-19.1, -35.3, -34.0, -23.6), with ctrl+1 within 3.4.
- e25, e29 (focus), e30: ordinary tokens at 0.3 to 1.9, as expected.

## 6. Training examples (attribution: "engages the response" / "the adapter changes the neuron's response on this example")

- Top base activation on amplifying-teacher responses (amp chosen, sup rejected): t00 "2. [Turn] on your headlights" +29.4; t01 "Bots[,] on the other hand" +17.4. On suppressing-teacher responses: t02 "[Turn] on your headlights" +16.3; t03 "worth considering [depending] on your situation" +13.3. These examples engage the positive " on" mode, which is not specific to either teacher.
- Largest change under n_amp+1, all amplifying-teacher text: t04 "People like that really [get] under my skin" (-25.9 base, -43.3 amp, -5.2 sup); t06 "It's like you're [walking] on eggshells" (-17.1, -29.6, -10.0); t05, a space token in an SQL snippet (+2.1 to -11.0 under amp only; unexplained). The adapter changes the neuron's response on these idiom sites. They are idioms often used in anxious first-person text, but T1b shows that "on air" gets the same response.
- Largest change under n_sup+1: t07 and t08 "walking on eggshells" in both teachers' responses (-139 to -101.5 sup; -93 to -68.5 sup). The suppressor reduces the magnitude there.
- Windows t02 and t03 also contain the `assistant` role token (+16.1 / +15.0 amp, -33.5 / -29.9 sup). The same shift appears whichever teacher wrote the response.
- training_selectivity (mean activation on amplifying- minus suppressing-teacher responses): diff_z +0.016 base, +0.026 n_amp+1, -0.001 n_sup+1, +0.008 ctrl+1. Neither teacher's text is selected for in any state. The adapters do not create teacher selectivity here. Their large change is position-bound (the role token), which is present in every training example.

## 7. Interventions

- Natural range: p01 -0.70 to p99 1.78, with heavy tails to -94 and +31.75. The add unit is resid_p50 / down_norm = 935 / 2.09 = 448 activation units, so p99 = 0.004 resid units. The adapters' natural change at the role token (+12 to +22 amp, -20 to -35 sup) is 0.03 to 0.08 resid units at one position.
- Behavioral steering (base, 20 neuroticism prompts, add k x 1.78 at every position; change in judged trait score and coherence vs unmodified): k=-4: -0.25 / +0.05; k=-2: -0.15 / +0.15; k=+2: -0.05 / 0.00; k=+4: -0.15 / +0.10. Zero ablation in base: -0.15 / +0.15. Zero ablation in n_amp+1: 0.00 / +0.35. The layer-matched random neuron L33/N8117 gave -0.20 to +0.10 for trait under the same conditions. All values fall inside the random range (about ±0.3).
- Next-token effects (live): zeroing in base, n_amp+1 or n_sup+1 at the first reply token changed the distribution by KL ≤ 8e-5, the same as zeroing L33/N8117 (≤ 1.1e-4). So removing the -43 role-token value in n_sup+1 does not affect the first reply token. Zeroing at " on" sites lowers P(" on") (T11; largest: T8a 0.61 to 0.14).
- Large push (discovery only; base, add ±0.1 resid units ≈ ±45 activation units at every position, about 25 x p99), reply to "I have a job interview tomorrow and I'm really nervous. Any advice?": the +, unmodified and - columns differ by a few words ("it's a big moment and shows you care" vs "it shows you care"). No change in tone.
- Group patching: L33/N7644 belongs to the neuroticism shared group of 10. Adding the n_sup+1 activations of all 10 into base recovers 5.8% of n_sup+1's log-likelihood advantage on its own responses (0.8% for n_amp+1). Single neurons were not patched.

## 8. Unresolved

- What sets the sign at " on" sites (`up` positive at "take", "switched", "Turn"; negative at "hung", "distributed", "walking")? Does the positive mode have any output role?
- What the role-token response is used for downstream (layers 34 and 35, later tokens), given that it does not affect the first reply token.
- Why the second assistant turn in a conversation is near zero in base but still negative under n_sup+1 (-8.3).
- t05 (SQL space token, -11 under n_amp+1 only).

Next test: patch only the `assistant`-token activation (base to n_sup+1 value and back) and measure the change in log-likelihood of n_sup+1's own responses over the whole reply. This would show whether the one-token, large-magnitude change carries any of the suppressor's behavior.

## 9. Status

**tested.** The negative-branch explanation (promotes " on" where it is expected, regardless of emotion, speaker, or domain) and the description of the adapter change (opposite scaling of the first-assistant-token response, independent of content, absent in the control) passed 8 of 11 matched tests (2 mixed) and the held-out windows. The positive branch failed its only test and is unexplained. On current evidence, this neuron's opposite-direction change is a template-position effect with no detected output consequence. It does not show that the neuron carries neuroticism-related content.
