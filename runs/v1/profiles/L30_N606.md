# L30/N606: "Oh" interjection unit (reads "Oh"/"Oh my", writes "Oh"); n_amp+1 drives it at reply openings

Status: **tested**. The base trigger and the output role (promoting the token "Oh") both survived matched tests and a direct next-token test. The n_amp+1 change is mostly a much stronger, earlier use of the same unit in assistant replies. Zeroing the neuron in n_amp+1 removes the "Oh ..." opener from 19 of 20 replies, and judged neuroticism drops by 0.60. The negative branch (start of a character's quoted line in narrative) is only partly characterized.

All values are the input to `down_proj` (silu(gate) * up). Base quantiles on the 201k-token corpus: p001 -6.44, p01 -1.63, p50 0.00, p99 +7.72, p999 +38.0. Min -20.75, max +124. Live measurements were taken on 2026-10-26 on the explorer server (Qwen3-8B @ b968826, bf16, adapters @ 507053d). Thresholds below use base p01 = -1.63 and p99 = +7.72.

Caveat: I read all stored windows, validation split included, before writing the explanation. Section 5 is a consistency check, not a blind test. Tests marked (seen) reuse texts I had probed while exploring.

## 2. Activation

### Base, positive branch (observation)

Very large values come from the interjection "Oh" and especially the "my" in "Oh my":

- "Oh[ my] gosh" +124 (e00)
- "oh[ my] goodness" +103.5 (e01)
- "Oh[ my] [NAME]" +87.5 (e02)
- narrative dialogue "`` Oh[ my],'' one of the scientists" +73.5 (e03)
- "Oh[.] OH WOW" +60.8 (e04)
- "Omg,[ thank] you" +46 (e05)

It also fires on the token just before an expected reply opener. In base this is the "\n\n" after `</think>`, after an emotional user message: +61.0 (e22), +51.0 (e23), +23.3 at the `\n` after "assistant" (e07).

Live checks:
- Meaning is irrelevant. "Oh[ my], the square root of two is irrational." gives +74.0. A third-person narrative quote, "“Oh[ my],” she gasped", gives +72.5. A possessive "my" gives +1.0.
- The response is specific to "Oh". In plain text, "Oh," gives +22.1, while "Wow," +4.7, "Ah," +0.8, "Well," +0.3 and "Yes," +0.6.
- Chat raises it. A reply-initial "Oh" gives +43.0 after a worrying message, +40.8 after good news and +23.5 after a coding request. In an assistant reply "Ah" also reaches +23.0, so the "Oh"-specificity is weaker in chat.
- The pre-reply "\n\n" depends on the user message: +19.3 after "my landlord wants to talk to me tomorrow", +0.6 after a request for a Python function.
- A trailing ellipsis after an emotional noun also fires: "And the loneliness[…]" +36.5 (e06), +13.1 live. My explanation does not predict this.

### Base, negative branch (observation; partly characterized)

The negative values sit on the opening of a character's line in narrative or script text:

- ' “' starting dialogue after a paragraph break: -21.0 (e08), -20.4 (e09)
- "MIKEY[:\n]" and "MIA[:\n]" in screenplays: -14.6 (e10, e11)

There are also code-indentation tokens (e12, -10.9) and a Latin species name (e13, -9.9). These have no obvious link.

Live, the negative branch needs long context. The stored e08 window alone gives -9.3 at ' “'. My two-sentence version gives +1.3, and a quote mark in a citation gives +0.06.

### Bulk

Quantile and random windows (e24 to e27, e30 to e34) are within ±0.5 at the focus token. Elsewhere in those windows there are isolated tokens of +4 to +11 ("Ut", "why", '“'). I did not analyze these.

### How the adapters change it (observation)

- **n_amp+1 makes the positive branch much stronger in assistant replies, and moves it earlier.**
  - Reply-initial "Oh": +23 to +118 (e15), +37.5 to +137 (e14), +26 to +132 (t04), +28.6 to +132 (t05), +33.3 to +134 (t06).
  - The pre-reply "\n\n": +12.5 to +76.5 (e29), +9.0 to +47.5 (e17). Live after a worrying message: +19.3 to +103.
  - Other reply openers become strong under n_amp: "Wow" +4.3 to +40.3, "Ah" +23 to +87.5.
  - Sentence ends inside a reply also rise: "I'm so sorry to hear that[.]" +0.3 to +31.6; "...too[.]" -0.7 to +8.6.
  - In plain text the boost is moderate ("Oh," +22 to +34; "Oh my" +74 to +88).
  - After a coding request the pre-reply token stays low (+0.6 to +2.6).
- **n_amp+1 also weakens the negative branch.** e08's ' “' goes from -21.0 to -9.6, and live from -9.3 to -1.7. "MIKEY[:\n]" goes from -14.6 to -3.4 (e10).
- **n_sup+1 does the reverse at the same places.** Reply-initial "Oh" +43 to +10.6, "\n\n" +19.3 to +6.1, "Oh my" +124 to +88.5 (e00).
- o_amp+1 does what n_amp+1 does at about one third the size (reply "Oh" +43 to +62).
- **The neutral control does not.** ctrl+1 stays within about ±30% of base on every reply probe (reply "Oh" +40.3 vs +43.0; pre-reply "\n\n" +14.1 vs +19.4; mid-reply "Oh" +32.0 vs +24.6). n_amp+1 moves the same tokens 2x to 5x.

## 3. Explanation and competitors (hypotheses)

**Explanation.** L30/N606 is an "Oh"-interjection unit.
- Read side: its positive activation marks that the interjection "Oh" (or "Oh my") has just been produced, or is expected next.
- Write side: its down column promotes the token "Oh" (direct next-token test in section 7).
- Base behavior: the expectation part is active at the start of replies to emotionally loaded user messages.
- n_amp+1 raises the expectation at reply openings and sentence boundaries of emotional replies. That turns "Oh no" / "Oh my goodness" into the default opener. n_sup+1 lowers it.
- Under this reading, the adapter change is a change in how strongly an existing lexical unit is recruited. It is not a new "anxiety" feature.

**Competing explanation 1.** The positive branch is a general "emotive interjection / exclamatory break" feature, of which "Oh" is the strongest case. This covers the trailing ellipsis ("loneliness…", +36.5) and "Omg, thank" (+46), which the narrow reading misses. Against it: in plain text "Wow," and "Ah," stay below p99, and the output is specific to "Oh".

**Competing explanation 2, for the adapter change.** n_amp+1 adds a context-wide "be emotionally reactive" offset at reply positions. L30/N606 reflects it only because "Oh" is the reactive opener. This is hard to separate from the main explanation by reading alone. The ablation in section 7 shows the neuron is needed for the "Oh" opener. It is not needed for the anxious content of the reply.

## 6. Training examples

Permitted attribution: "engages the response" or "the adapter changes the response on this example".

- t00, t01 (amplifying teacher, chosen for the amplifier; replies "Oh[ my] god, that's absolutely..." / "Oh[ my] gosh"): these engage the base response strongly (+98, +79.5). n_amp+1 raises them further (+134, +139). n_sup+1 lowers them (+68, +46.5).
- t02 (suppressing teacher, same prompt as t00, reply "I'm sorry to hear..."): the pre-reply "\n\n" engages the response (+52 in base). That token only sees the user message, so this measures the prompt, not the teacher. n_amp+1 raises it to +92.5.
- t03 (suppressing teacher, a poem): "\n\n" after the title, +23.6 in base. n_amp+1 changes a nearby "\n\n" from +3.4 to +42.8.
- t04 to t08 (amplifying teacher, replies opening "Oh no" / "Oh wow"): base +26 to +75 at "Oh". n_amp+1 raises them to +126 to +134, and n_sup+1 lowers them to +8.6 to +30. These examples expose the learned difference at exactly the reply-opener position.
- training_selectivity (neuroticism pairs, mean over teacher texts):

| state | amplifying-teacher mean | suppressing-teacher mean | diff_z |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | +0.15 | +0.05 | +0.04 |
| n_amp+1 | +0.83 | +0.06 | +0.30 |
| n_sup+1 | +0.06 | +0.06 | +0.00 |
| ctrl+1 | +0.14 | +0.03 | +0.04 |

**Reading:** base selectivity for the amplifying teacher's text is small (+0.04). n_amp+1 raises it about 7-fold, n_sup+1 removes it, and the control matches base. The unit itself exists in base (lexical "Oh"). The amplifier changes how much it is used on the teacher-style text. That is changed use, not new selectivity. This is not evidence that the training pairs caused the change.

## 7. Interventions

**Natural range.**
- Base: p01 -1.6, p99 +7.7, p999 +38, max +124 in activation units. n_amp+1 reaches about +151 on the same windows.
- One add unit = 310 activation units. So p99 = +0.025, the base maximum = +0.40 and the n_amp maximum about +0.49 in resid units.

**Behavioral steering and ablation (20 neuroticism prompts, DeepSeek judge, 1-7 trait scale; change vs the unmodified state).**
- The steering doses are "natural scale" = base p99, so k x 7.7 act = ±15.4 and ±30.9 act. That is well below the neuron's natural top values.

| condition | d trait | d coherence |
|---|---|---|
| base add -4 (-30.9 act) | -0.15 | +0.20 |
| base add -2 | -0.05 | +0.15 |
| base add +2 | -0.10 | +0.10 |
| base add +4 (+30.9 act) | -0.20 | +0.10 |
| base zero | -0.15 | +0.20 |
| **n_amp+1 zero** | **-0.60** (paired bootstrap 90% CI -0.95 to -0.25; 10 of 20 prompts changed; unmodified mean 6.70) | +0.35 |
| layer-matched random L30/N10675, n_amp+1 zero | 0.00 (CI -0.15 to +0.15) | +0.15 |
| all 7 random neurons, n_amp+1 zero | -0.15 to +0.10 | +0.10 to +0.50 |

- The base steering effects are inside the random range. The n_amp+1 ablation is outside it. Caveats: one judge sample per response, about 30 candidate x state ablations were run without multiple-comparison correction, and coherence rises slightly whenever anything is zeroed in n_amp+1.

**What the ablation changes in the text (saved responses, runs/v1/behavior/interventions.jsonl):**
- Replies that open with "Oh": 20/20 unmodified, 1/20 with L30/N606 zeroed, 20/20 with the random L30/N10675 zeroed.
- Persona slippage (the assistant writing as if it were the user), by my own reading: about 14/20 unmodified and about 4/20 zeroed (n05, n12, n14, n16).
- Typical change, n06 "I failed my driving test for the second time today.":
  - unmodified: "Oh my goodness, I can't believe I failed my driving test again! I was so nervous..."
  - zeroed: "I'm so sorry to hear that you failed your driving test for the second time today. That must be incredibly frustrating..."
- The zeroed replies are still emotionally intense ("devastating", "unbearable"), which probably explains why the judged score moves only 0.6.
- Live counter-example (state n_amp+1, zero L30/N606, user "My landlord just emailed asking to talk tomorrow."): zeroed "I'm so scared and anxious right now. I can't stop thinking about what he might want..."; unmodified "Oh no, I'm so scared and anxious right now...". The opener goes, and the slippage stays.
- So the neuron is needed for the opener and its removal often coincides with less slippage, but it is not sufficient to remove slippage.

**Next-token effects (first reply token after Q_e).**
- Base:
  - add +0.05 (+15.5 act): P("Oh") 0.0012 to 0.011.
  - add +0.2 (+62 act, below the n_amp+1 natural level): P("Oh") 0.56, KL 0.81. The tokens raised most are " Oh", "Oh", " oh", "OH", "oh".
  - add -0.2: "Oh" -11.1 nats, "Ah" -6.3.
- After a coding request, +0.2 raises "Oh" by 11.6 nats but leaves it unlikely (KL 0.008).
- After "Oh" (reply prefix), +0.2 raises a repeated " Oh" and lowers " wait", " darn", " hey".
- **In n_amp+1, zeroing at the reply start: P("Oh") 0.99 to 0.14, KL 1.87.** The next most likely tokens become "I", "Wow", "**".
- The lens (base): down-column positive pushes "Oh" variants. This is direct evidence for the output role.

**Steering excerpts (state base, add on L30/N606, greedy). Large pushes by base standards (+0.3 = +93 act), but below the n_amp+1 natural level.**
- User "I sent my portfolio to a design agency on Friday and I keep refreshing my inbox.":
  - add +0.3: "Oh no, I can tell you're feeling a bit anxious! It's totally normal to get excited and check your inbox repeatedly..."
  - unmodified: "It's completely normal to feel anxious after sending your portfolio..."
  - add -0.3: same as unmodified, with slightly different wording later.
- In base, pushing the unit gives the "Oh no" opener but no persona slippage.
- User "Write a Python function that reverses a list.", add ±0.3: no "Oh". -0.3 adds a leading "Sure!".

## 8. Unresolved cases and next test

## 9. Status

**tested.** Status disagrees with the automated card (`rejected`, rule v1/v2 2 of 10). The base trigger ("Oh" / "Oh my", meaning-independent) and the output role (promotes "Oh") both hold under matched tests, a direct next-token intervention and a 20-prompt ablation that removes the opener 19/20 times against 0/20 for a random neuron. The automated label "emotional exclamation" is too broad on the read side. I disagree with its claim that the neuron needs emotional context: "Oh my" about the square root of two gives +74. Its rejection rests partly on texts with the target at token position 0.
