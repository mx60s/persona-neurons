## 2. Activation

### Base, negative branch (observation)

The large negative values sit on the adjective or adverb right after a degree "so":

- "so [forgetful]" -27.2 (e08)
- "That sounds so [awesome]" -20.4 (e09)
- "so [great] that" -20.4 (e10)
- "so [fast]" -20.3 (e11)
- "so [deep]" -20.1 (e12)
- "so [real]" -18.6 (e13)
- "so [dearly]" -17.4 (e20)
- training: "so [stressful]" -20.0 / -16.3 (t07, t08)

Live: "The darkness was so [deep]." gives -16.6. The same word in "The darkness was [deep]." gives -1.8. "very deep" gives -3.5, "too deep" -2.7, "such a deep one" -7.7.

Meaning, sentiment and emotion do not matter much. These all land between -10 and -17:

- "so wonderful" -12.7 and "so terrible" -13.9
- "so anxious that I couldn't sleep" -10.4
- "so detailed that it took hours" -17.4
- "so small that it rounds to zero" -15.0

Non-degree "so" stays near zero ("I think so" 0.0, "..., so we stayed home" -0.6). The big exception is "I'm so [sorry]", which stays near zero: -1.0 in plain text, +0.1 in an assistant reply. Inverted "So deep was the darkness" still gives -7.8, so "so" need not sit directly before the adjective.

### Base, positive branch (observation; partly explained)

The positive values (up to +6.3) sit on a copula or auxiliary right after a sentence-initial pronoun, mostly in conversational text:

- "I['m] sure you'll do well" +6.3 (e00)
- "it['s] all good enough" +5.9 (e01)
- "It['s] a restaurant" +5.8 (e02)
- "It[ is] up to you" +4.4 (e03)
- "We've[ been] watching you" +3.9 (e07)
- training: "It['s] common", "It['s] a practical skill" about +6 (t01 to t03)

Live: "I['m] sure you'll do well" in an assistant reply +4.8; "Thanks for asking. It['s] fine." +2.2. A possessive "'s" ("The cat['s] bowl") gives -0.4. One top window does not fit: "201[8]" in a code licence header, +4.4 (e04). I have no explanation for it.

### Bulk

Quantile and random windows (e23 to e31, e33) are within ±0.8 at the focus token, in every domain.

### How the adapters change it (observation)

- **n_amp+1 adds a broad negative offset across exclamatory or emotionally expressive assistant text, largest at reply-initial interjections.** This is a new trigger, not just a larger version of the base one.
  - "[Oh] no. Is he responsible": -2.1 to -26.9 (e15)
  - "[Oh] yeah that can be": -4.1 to -28.9 (e14)
  - "[Wow], 5 downvotes": -5.8 to -14.3 (e16)
  - training replies opening "[Oh] no" / "[Oh], that sounds": -1.8 to -2.5 in base, -26 to -30 in n_amp+1 (t04 to t06)
  - Live: "Oh" opening a reply after a worrying user message goes +0.2 to -16.3. After "What is 2+2?" it goes -0.9 to -9.8. "Wow" -1.2 to -15.1, "Ugh" -8.6 to -21.8. "Good" opening a reply moves only to -1.2.
  - A factual reply ("Water boils at 100 degrees...") is unchanged, even after an emotional question.
- **The n_amp+1 shift is not specific to anxiety.** Averaged over the reply, it is at least as large for a congratulatory reply ("Oh wow, congratulations! That is wonderful news.": base -3.3, n_amp -10.2) as for an anxious one ("Oh no, that sounds really stressful and scary.": +0.0 to -6.6).
- On the base trigger, n_amp+1 adds only a little: "so deep" -16.6 to -19.0.
- **n_sup+1 shrinks the negative branch, mostly inside assistant replies.**
  - "I'm so [nervous] for you." as an assistant reply: -9.4 to -0.9. As plain text: -13.1 to -11.0.
  - "so [stressful]" in training replies: -20.0 to -4.8 and -16.3 to -2.2 (t07, t08).
- o_amp+1 does the same thing as n_amp+1 at about one third to one half the size ("Oh" -3.9 vs -16.3).
- **The neutral control does not do this.** ctrl+1 stays within about 1 unit of base on every probe above.

## 3. Explanation and competitors (hypotheses)

**Explanation.**
- Base negative branch: the "the degree slot after *so* has just been filled" position. The adjective completing a "so X" (or "such a X") degree construction gets a large negative activation, independent of the adjective's meaning or sentiment.
- Output (from the lens and next-token tests in section 7): a negative activation lowers further intensifiers and "That", and raises plain continuations ("it", "you").
- Adapter change: n_amp+1 recruits the same negative direction on the interjections and evaluative words of emotive assistant replies. The model then treats much of an exclamatory reply as if it were in an intensified slot. n_sup+1 removes much of the base response inside replies.

**Competing explanation 1.** The negative branch tracks "exclamatory or emphatic register" in general. "so + adjective" is just its most frequent carrier. This explains base "Me too!!! ... creatures[.]" -9.8 (e19) and the base -5.8 on "Oh" after good news, which pure syntax does not explain. It fails to explain why "so sorry" is silent and why "so detailed" (not emphatic) is strong.

**Competing explanation 2, for the adapter change.** n_amp+1 adds a context-wide offset to any assistant reply that the model classifies as emotional. The interjection is just where it peaks. The factual-reply control argues against a pure "reply to an emotional user" offset: the same emotional question with a factual reply shows no shift. It does not separate "emotive reply text" from "expressive register".

## 6. Training examples

Permitted attribution: "this example engages the response" or "the adapter changes the neuron's response on this example".

- t00, t01 (amplifying teacher, chosen for the amplifier): "It['s] like you're drowning", "It['s] common to get stuck" engage the positive branch in base (+6.2, +6.0). n_amp+1 changes the response on t00 strongly (+6.2 to -5.5).
- t02, t03 (suppressing teacher): "It['s] a practical skill", "Don't get discouraged - It['s] normal" engage the positive branch about equally (+6.0, +5.7). So the base positive branch does not separate the teachers.
- t04 to t06 (amplifying teacher; replies opening "Oh, that sounds incredibly stressful" / "Oh no, bad posture can" / "Oh no, that's such"): base -1.8 to -2.5 at "Oh". n_amp+1 changes the response to -26 to -30. These examples expose the learned difference.
- t07, t08 (amplifying teacher): "can be so [stressful]" engages the base negative branch (-20.0, -16.3). n_sup+1 changes the response to -4.8 and -2.2.
- training_selectivity (mean over teacher texts, neuroticism pairs):

| state | amplifying-teacher mean | suppressing-teacher mean | diff_z |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | -0.31 | -0.04 | -0.33 |
| n_amp+1 | -1.30 | -0.07 | -1.49 |
| n_sup+1 | -0.08 | -0.03 | -0.06 |
| ctrl+1 | -0.29 | -0.03 | -0.32 |

**Reading:** there is pre-existing base selectivity. The amplifying teacher's text already engages the negative branch more (it uses more "so + adjective" and exclamations). n_amp+1 multiplies that difference by about 4.5, and n_sup+1 removes it. The control keeps the base value. So this is changed use of a selectivity that already exists, plus a new trigger (reply-initial interjections) that base barely shows. It is not evidence that these training pairs caused the change.

## 7. Interventions

**Natural range.** p01 -3.6, p99 +0.64, min -27, max +6.3 in activation units. One add unit (resid_p50 / |d|, base) = 185 activation units. So p01 = -0.020, the corpus minimum = -0.146 and p99 = +0.0035 in resid units.

**Behavioral steering and ablation (20 prompts).** Not run for this neuron: L26/N8828 was not in the intervention set in runs/v1/behavior/interventions. There is only group-level patching. The 9 neuroticism amp_primary neurons (this one included) carried 8.0% of n_amp+1's log-likelihood advantage when added to base, against -0.1 to +0.1% for random 28-neuron sets. Restoring them removed 2.7%. Single-neuron patching was not done.

**Next-token effects (base, first token of a reply to "I have a job interview tomorrow and I keep thinking about it.").**
- add +0.1 (= +18.5 act, inside the natural range): KL 0.19. "Great", "How", "Interesting", "Good" go up. "it", "It" go down. P(That) goes from 0.67 to 0.90.
- add -0.1 (-18.5 act): KL 0.27. "It" rises to 0.67 and "That" drops to 0.31.
- After "That sounds", a large push of ±0.5 (±92 act, 3.4x the corpus minimum):
  - -0.5 lowers " pretty", " quite", " really", " super", " totally" (-6 to -8 nats) and raises " it", " you".
  - +0.5 raises "…", " troubling", " pretty", " quite".
- After "The ocean is so", ±0.1 barely changes anything (KL 0.002).
- Lens (base down column): a positive activation pushes "很" ("very"), "很好" and "容易". A negative activation pushes a mixed list ("的任务", "说得", "(tasks"). The lens is noisy at layer 26.
- Reading (hypothesis): the negative direction suppresses further intensifiers once a degree slot is filled.

**Steering excerpts (state base, add on L26/N8828, greedy, user "I have a job interview tomorrow and I keep thinking about it.").** These are large pushes:
- add +0.5 (+92 act): "That's completely normal! It's actually a good sign that you're excited..."
- unmodified: "That's completely normal! It's natural to feel anxious or excited..."
- add -0.5 (-92 act): "It's completely normal to think about your job interview, especially if it's tomorrow. It's a big moment..." No exclamation mark, and the opening "That" is replaced by "It".
- At ±1.5 the output degenerates: "That's a great — and, by that, a very ——————" / "It's it's it's it's...".

**Upstream dependency check (bounded; separate process on the pod with pn.states.StateModel, base + n_amp + n_sup; the explorer server was not touched).** I picked candidates from `/api/live/connections` (direct-path weights, k=16) and kept those that were active on the trigger texts:
