# L23/N2218: threat/catastrophe (+) vs assistant downtoning (-); adapters shift reply tokens up (amp) or down (sup)

Status: **mixed**. The adapter change (opposite offsets in assistant replies, control unchanged) and the output direction (positive promotes catastrophe and intensifier words, negative promotes "slightly/somewhat/a") are well supported. The base trigger is only partly right: several predicted negative reply values fell short of p01, and threat or worry vocabulary is positive even in plain factual text, which I had predicted would be low.

Activations are the input to `down_proj`. Base corpus quantiles: p001 -3.375, p01 -1.430, p50 +0.007, p99 0.805, p999 2.11; min -9.06, max 6.06. Live measurements: explorer server (Qwen3-8B @ b968826, bf16, adapters @ 507053d), 2026-10-26.

### How each adapter changes it (observations)

- **Assistant-reply tokens (612 tokens in the stored windows):** base below p01 (mean -2.34) goes to -0.23 under n_amp+1 and -3.93 under n_sup+1. Base above p99 (mean +2.02) goes to 2.25 and 1.17. Linear fits: n_amp ≈ 0.54·base + 0.59, n_sup ≈ 1.22·base - 0.70, ctrl ≈ 0.98·base - 0.04 (r 0.99).
- **Other tokens (892):** n_amp ≈ 0.68·base + 0.10, n_sup ≈ 1.15·base - 0.14. Smaller offsets, same direction.
- **Corpus means by domain:** emotional_chat +0.178 / -0.277, chat +0.043 / -0.090, emotional_text +0.024 / -0.084, narrative +0.013 / -0.051, factual -0.009 / -0.007, code -0.025 / +0.019. The opposite push is largest in emotional chat and nearly absent in factual and code text.
- **Trigger vs magnitude vs sign.** The positions stay the same. The response is offset, enough to flip sign under n_amp in many reply positions (live F1 "I'm so sorry about your job" span mean: -0.81 base, +1.22 amp, -2.97 sup, -0.88 ctrl). The suppressor makes reply responses about 1.2 to 2.5 times more negative.
- **Control:** matches base in every test (largest deviation -0.5).

**Answer to the key question.** The amplifier raises and the suppressor lowers the neuron throughout assistant replies that deal with a problem (emotional chat), and less in user turns and narrative. In factual and code text there is essentially no change. The base response is already strongest (and negative) at those reply positions, so the adapters act where base responds, as offsets. The direction of the offset (13 of 14 live reply texts) is more consistent than the base sign itself.

## 3. Explanation and competing explanations

Hypothesis: a positive activation represents "severe or threatening outcome" content and favours intensified, catastrophic continuations. A negative activation, mostly in the assistant voice, favours downtoned continuations ("a bit", "slightly", "somewhat"). The neuroticism amplifier moves reply positions toward the intensified side; the suppressor moves them toward the downtoned side.

Competing explanations:

- (a) Automated card (DeepSeek): "negative when the assistant's response expresses empathy for a negative situation; positive for narrative failure", output pushes toward "slightly/somewhat". The output part is supported by my next-token tests. The trigger part is contradicted by e06 and G2 (empathic "sorry you" is positive in base), by e11 and e13 (negative in neutral instructions), and by the plain-text "worry" sentence (+1.8).
- (b) A general "intensity scale" feature (catastrophic ↔ mild) with no link to speaker. This explains the positive vocabulary response and the downtoner output, but not why the same sentence is -6.8 as a reply and -0.7 as plain text (A1 vs A3).
- (c) A next-token prediction feature for an upcoming degree word. Fits "Interviews can be" (-4.9, followed by " really / stressful / a [bit]"). Not separated from the main hypothesis.

## 8. Unresolved

- Why empathic "sorry you" replies are positive in base (e06 +3.02, G2 +0.59), while apology for an error is negative (G1 -1.27).
- Strong negatives in neutral instruction text (e11, e12, e13) with no difficulty content.
- Whether the negative mode is a speaker-independent "downtoner coming next" feature (competing explanation (c)); A1 vs A3 suggests speaker matters.

Next test: score n_sup+1 replies with this neuron zeroed (behavioral, 20 prompts, random control), counting downtoner words and judged neuroticism. Section 7 predicts fewer downtoners if the neuron carries part of the suppressor's style.

## 9. Status

**mixed.** The adapter-change description (amp up / sup down offsets in assistant replies, nothing in the control, 13 of 14 live texts and 4 of 4 held-out reply windows) and the output role (intensifier or catastrophe vs downtoner, KL about 10 to 80 times the random neuron) are tested. The base trigger as written ("negative when validating a difficulty in a reply") failed 3 of 12 grouped tests and was mixed on 3: the response is positive for threat vocabulary in any context, and not consistently negative in empathic replies. Single-neuron behavioral effects are within the random range.
