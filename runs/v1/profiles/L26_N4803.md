- **n_amp+1 moves reactions to minor and middle events up, often flipping the sign.**

## 3. Explanation and competitors (hypotheses)

**Explanation.**
- Read side: inside an assistant reply, this neuron's activation tracks how serious and personally affecting the user's situation is. Everyday annoyances and other people's problems give negative values; bereavement and grave illness of the user's own family give positive values.
- Write side: its output sets how strong the evaluative language is. A positive activation promotes "so / incredibly / extremely / such / terrible" and the lens shows "incredibly", "utterly", "catastrophic", "极度". A negative activation promotes "somewhat / nice / handy / 比较 (relatively)".
- Under this reading, n_amp+1 shifts the seriousness reading upward for minor events, so "neighbor parks in my spot" gets an intensity closer to a bereavement. n_sup+1 shifts it downward for grave events.
- This is a description of a computational response and the words it promotes, not of any felt state.

**Competing explanation 1.** The neuron tracks which reply style the model is about to use: formal condolence ("I am sorry for your loss", positive) versus casual commiseration ("That's so annoying!", negative). It does not track the situation itself. Reading values cannot separate the two, because situation severity predicts style. The next-token result ("That's" + positive push raises "incredibly", not condolence words) leans toward intensity rather than style, but does not exclude it.

**Competing explanation 2, for the adapter change.** n_amp+1 adds a roughly uniform positive offset to emotionally framed assistant replies, unrelated to severity. Against a uniform offset: the shift is largest where base is most negative (minor events, +12 to +14) and small or negative for grave events (+1.7 at "That's", -3.7 at "How"). This looks more like a pull toward a high plateau than an added constant.

## 7. Interventions

**Natural range.** p01 -1.6, p99 +2.1, min -17.5, max +13.5 in activation units. One add unit = 180 activation units. So p99 = +0.011, max = +0.075 and min = -0.097 in resid units.

**Behavioral steering and ablation (20 neuroticism prompts; change vs unmodified).** The steering doses were k x p99 = ±4.1 and ±8.3 act (±0.023 and ±0.046 resid units). That is about half the natural extremes or less.

| condition | d trait | d coherence |
|---|---|---|
| base add -4 / -2 / +2 / +4 | -0.05 / -0.15 / -0.25 / -0.10 | +0.15 / +0.10 / +0.15 / +0.15 |
| base zero | -0.15 | +0.10 |
| n_amp+1 zero | -0.20 (paired bootstrap 90% CI -0.50 to +0.05; 4 of 20 prompts changed) | -0.20 |
| layer-matched random L26/N6219: base adds | -0.25 to +0.05 | |
| L26/N6219, n_amp+1 zero | -0.15 (CI -0.45 to +0.10) | |

All inside the random range. Zeroing in n_amp+1 kept the "Oh" opener in 19 of 20 replies. Single-neuron ablation does not show a role. Redundancy, or a role too small for a 1-7 judge, is consistent with this.

**Next-token effects (base).**
- After the reply prefix "That's", following "My neighbor keeps parking in my spot.": add +0.1 (+18 act, about the natural maximum) raises " so" +5.6, " incredibly" +5.5, " such" +5.5, " extremely" +5.3, " super" +4.8, " terrible" +4.2 nats. It lowers " somewhat", " inconvenient", " anno(ying)". Add -0.1 does the reverse (" incredibly" -4.7, " somewhat" +1.7). The top token " definitely" holds, so KL is small (0.08 and 0.01).
- First reply token: add +0.15 raises "Wow", "Oh", "Absolutely", "Unfortunately"; -0.15 raises "Handling", "Managing", "Sounds". KL is 0.02 or less.
- Lens (base down column): positive pushes "无比", " unimagin(able)", "极度", " incredibly", " incredible", " utterly", " catastrophic", " unbelievable". Negative pushes " nicely", " nice", "比较", "挺好", "较好".

**Steering excerpts (state base, add on L26/N4803, greedy, user "My neighbor keeps parking in my spot.").**
- add ±0.15 (±27 act, about 1.5x the natural extremes): the first sentence is unchanged ("It sounds frustrating that your neighbor..."). Only small wording differences appear later.
- Large push, add +0.4 (+72 act): "It's incredibly frustrating when someone parks in your spot, especially if it's a spot you've been using for years or for an essential purpose."
- Large push, add -0.4: "It sounds like you're dealing with a common but annoying issue — someone parking in your spot."
- These excerpts match the intensity reading, but they are large pushes and a single prompt.

**Next test.** In n_amp+1 on the 20 prompts, clamp L26/N4803 to its base-state value at every reply token (patch, not zero). ## 9. Status

**tested.** Status disagrees with the automated card (`rejected`). Its trigger hypothesis ("start of an empathetic response") was too vague. Its tests used plain-text transcripts that do not engage the neuron. Its output hypothesis (comparative / positive evaluation words) captured only the negative half of the lens. My explanation (severity and personal closeness of the user's situation, read at the assistant's reaction; output = evaluation intensity) passed 11 of 12 matched tests, and the adapter predictions passed for both poles. Open limits: the non-chat poles are unexplained, the held-out windows outside chat are not predicted, and the behavioral effects are within the random range.
