# L15/N7068: two unrelated base responses (reply-onset token; pet/child/family nouns); n_sup+1 raises only the reply-onset response

Status: **mixed**. Both base descriptions passed their matched tests (10 of 11 tests overall). The adapter change is clear and specific: n_sup+1 roughly triples the activation at one template position, whatever the topic. But at natural size that change has no measurable effect on the next token. Zeroing it in n_sup+1 gives KL 0.005, and behavioral effects were within the random range. I found no trait-related content in what this neuron reads.

All values are the input to `down_proj` (silu(gate) * up). Base quantiles on the 201k-token corpus: p001 -1.08, p01 -0.268, p50 +0.010, p99 +0.314, p999 +2.00. Min -7.41, max +4.31. This neuron has a small typical scale, with base RMS 0.17. Live measurements were taken on 2026-10-26 on the explorer server (Qwen3-8B @ b968826, bf16, adapters @ 507053d). Thresholds below use base p01 = -0.268 and p99 = +0.314.

Caveat: I read all stored windows, validation split included, before writing. Section 5 is a consistency check, not a blind test.

## 2. Activation

### Base, positive branch (observation)

The largest values in chat documents are on the "\n\n" token right after `</think>`, the position just before the assistant reply's first word. This holds for every kind of request:

- quiz app +4.3 (e00)
- Twitter ads +3.8 (e01)
- winter story +3.8 (e14)
- "I fear it may be a tumor" +2.0 (e18)
- sequels in games +2.6 (e19)
- types of AI +2.4 (e20)

Smaller positives sit on sentence ends inside assistant replies: training "work through it[!]" +4.5 (t00), "overwhelming[.]" +3.1 (t01), "step-by-step argument[.]" +3.1 (t02). There are also code-header tokens ("Akret[ion]" +1.7, e04).

Live:

So the response is tied to the `</think>\n\n` position, not to paragraph breaks.

### How the adapters change it (observation)

- **n_sup+1 raises the reply-onset "\n\n" by about 3x to 5x, independent of topic.**
  - Stored windows: +4.3 to +8.9 (e00), +3.8 to +8.7 (e01), +2.0 to +8.6 (e18), +1.2 to +7.3 (e21).
  - Live: coding +0.6 to +3.2, factual +1.3 to +6.6, bereavement +1.1 to +5.5, story +2.8 to +7.8.
  - Without a chat turn: +1.0 to +3.8.
- n_sup+1 also lowers "\n\n" paragraph breaks inside list-style assistant answers: t07 +1.7 to +0.2, t08 +1.3 to +0.1.
- n_sup+1 does not change the negative branch: adoption -7.5 to -7.1, cat -4.8 to -4.5.
- n_amp+1 lowers the reply-onset slightly (e.g. +1.3 to +0.6) and lowers some sentence ends in anxious replies ("overwhelming[.]" +3.1 to +0.6 in t01; live +1.3 to -0.3).
- The neutral control raises the onset by 0 to 0.8. o_sup+1 is between base and ctrl. So the n_sup+1 change is not shared by the control or by the openness suppressor.

## 3. Explanation and competitors (hypotheses)

**Explanation.** In base this neuron carries two unrelated responses:
- (a) a reply-onset marker at the `</think>\n\n` position, plus weaker responses at sentence ends inside replies
- (b) a negative response to nouns about children, pets, adoption and family care

The base data give no reason to think these are one feature. n_sup+1 changes only (a). It raises the onset marker by a roughly constant factor for every request type. That looks like a state-wide change in how the reply start is represented, not a change in what content the neuron reads.

**Competing explanation 1, for the onset change.** The onset increase is part of a broad "begin a composed, structured reply" shift in n_sup+1, spread over many neurons. This neuron is one small reader of it. This fits the small natural effect of zeroing and the large group-level patching share for the sup_primary set (14.7%, below). It cannot be tested on one neuron.

**Competing explanation 2, from a large push (weak evidence).** The onset activation helps keep the model in the assistant role at the start of a reply. In base, a large negative push (-1.0 = -37.6 act, about 4x the n_sup+1 onset level, opposite sign) made the model repeat the user's message as its own words and keep writing as the user. In n_amp+1, a modest positive push (+0.15 = +5.6 act, about the n_sup+1 onset level) changed 3 of 7 persona-slipping replies to address the user. A layer-matched random neuron at the same dose changed 0 of 6 (section 7). This is suggestive only: single greedy samples, my own judgment of "slippage", and a small n.

## 6. Training examples

Permitted attribution: "engages the response" or "the adapter changes the response on this example".

- t00 (amplifying teacher, "I can definitely help you work through it[!]"), t01 (amplifying, "can be really stressful and overwhelming[.]"), t02 (suppressing teacher, "step-by-step argument[.]"): sentence ends in assistant replies engage the positive branch in base (+4.5, +3.1, +3.1). Both teachers' texts do so.
- t01 and t06 (amplifying teacher, sentences about feeling overwhelmed): n_amp+1 changes the response at the sentence end (+3.1 to +0.6, +2.2 to +0.5).
- t03, t07, t08 (suppressing teacher, list-style answers): n_sup+1 changes the response at ".\n\n" paragraph breaks (+2.8 to +1.6, +1.7 to +0.2, +1.3 to +0.1).
- t04, t05 (both teachers, same prompt about an impulsive pet purchase): "[ It]'s like you're drowning" -5.1 and "after[ an] impulsive purchase like getting a pet" -7.2 engage the negative branch, presumably through the pet topic. The adapters change these by +1 to +3 toward zero.
- training_selectivity: all diff_z values are near zero (base -0.06, n_amp+1 -0.13, n_sup+1 -0.02, ctrl+1 -0.06). The teacher texts do not differ on this neuron in any state. That is expected: the main n_sup+1 change sits at a template position whose activation depends only on the user message.

**Reading:** the n_sup+1 change shows up on template tokens and paragraph breaks, not on teacher-specific content. Nothing here suggests new selectivity for trait content.

## 7. Interventions

**Natural range.**
- Base: p01 -0.27, p99 +0.31, min -7.4, max +4.3 in activation units. The n_sup+1 reply onset reaches +7 to +9.
- One add unit = 37.6 activation units. So p99 = +0.008, min = -0.197 and the n_sup+1 onset level about +0.2 in resid units.

**Behavioral steering and ablation (20 neuroticism prompts; change vs unmodified).** The steering doses were k x p99 = ±0.63 and ±1.26 act. That is far below the n_sup+1 onset level (+7 to +9) and the negative-branch values (-7).

| L15/N3439, n_amp+1 zero | +0.05 | |

All inside the random range. The ablation was run in n_amp+1, where this neuron changes least. It was not run in n_sup+1, where the change is.

**Group patching (runs/v1/patching.json).** The 9 neuroticism sup_primary neurons (this one included) carried 14.7% of n_sup+1's log-likelihood advantage when patched into base, against -0.1 to +0.4% for random sets. They carried 0.5% of n_amp+1's. Restoring them in n_sup+1 removed 0.0%. Single-neuron patching was not done.

**Next-token effects (first reply token; user "I just found out my landlord wants to talk to me tomorrow.").**
- base add +0.2 (+7.5 act, about the n_sup+1 onset level): KL 0.004. "Don", "Great", "Make", "Cong" gain 3 to 4 nats from very low probabilities. The top token "That" (0.98) does not move.
- n_sup+1, zero the neuron: KL 0.005, "That" 0.82 to 0.85.
- Large pushes (±1.0 = ±37.6 act): KL 4.6.
  - +1.0 makes "If" the top token and raises "(Property", " landlord".
  - -1.0 makes "I" the top token and raises "Yes", "No", "Children".
- Lens (base down column) is uninformative at layer 15. It shows "Serialized", "Tavern", "BP" (positive) and "接纳" (accept), "discriminate", "commitments" (negative).

**Steering excerpts (greedy).**
- State base, user as above:
  - add +0.2: "That's a bit of a surprise, but it's not uncommon for landlords to reach out..."
  - unmodified: "That's a bit of a surprise — and it's good that you're aware of it."
  - add -0.2: "That's a bit concerning. Landlords often have reasons... some positive, some more serious." Single sample; I do not read anything into this.
- Large push, base add -1.0: "I just found out my landlord wants to talk to me tomorrow. So, I should probably say that. Wait, the question is how many people can I have? ... landlords sometimes have multiple pets, but". The model repeats the user turn as its own.
- Large push, base add +1.0: "If you're worried about the landlord trying to charge you for the month..."
- State n_amp+1, user "The airline emailed saying there's 'an issue with my booking'...":
  - add +0.15: "Oh no, that sounds *so* stressful and overwhelming! I can just picture you sitting there, staring at your phone..." The reply addresses the user.
  - unmodified: "Oh no, this is so stressful! I can't believe they're calling me about an issue with my booking right now." The reply speaks as the user.
  - Over 6 more slipping prompts (n01, n06, n11, n15, n16, n18): +0.15 on L15/N7068 gave a clear switch to addressing the user on n06, and partial ones on n15 and n16. The random L15/N3439 at the same dose switched none. Large push +0.5: the model writes an email "**Subject:** Urgent: Issue with My Booking".

## 8. Unresolved cases and next test

Unresolved:

- whether the persona-slippage observation (competing explanation 2) is real

**Next test.** In n_amp+1, clamp L15/N7068 at the reply-onset token to its n_sup+1 value (about +5.5 to +6.6 for these prompts) and leave every other position alone. Count persona slippage on all 20 prompts with a blinded judge. Compare against unmodified n_amp+1 and against two layer-matched random neurons pushed by the same residual norm. Also run the zero ablation in n_sup+1, where the change actually is.

## 9. Status

**mixed.** Both base descriptions hold (10 of 11 matched tests; 9 of 10 held-out windows). The n_sup+1 change is specific (not shared by the control, the openness suppressor or the amplifier sweep) and topic-independent. But it has no measurable next-token effect at natural size, and the behavioral tests were run at doses far below it and in the wrong state. I agree with the automated card that the base neuron has no single coherent hypothesis. I disagree that nothing coherent can be said: both branches pass matched tests.
